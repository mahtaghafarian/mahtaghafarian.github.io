Working Papers
JMP Air Pollution and Cardiovascular Mortality: Evidence from Thermal Inversions in the U.S.
Job Market Paper  ·  Work in Progress
Estimates the causal effect of PM2.5 on cardiovascular mortality at the county-month level using restricted NCHS mortality microdata linked to satellite-derived environmental measures. Systematically evaluates how thermal inversion construction choices affect instrument validity. Using the preferred specification (F-stat 463), a one-unit increase in PM2.5 raises cardiovascular mortality by 3.8%, ranging from 1.2 to 7.4% across specifications. Effects are larger for Black, female, elderly, and low-income populations. Results are robust to alternative pollutant measures, outcome transformations, and pre-pandemic samples.
Mental Health and Functional Consequences of Smoking: A Causal Approach Using Misreporting Corrections
Working Paper
Estimates the causal impacts of smoking on mental health and functional limitations using BRFSS data and cigarette tax variation. Addresses smoking status misreporting via a bivariate probit two-step model, showing that corrected estimates substantially exceed standard estimates.
Assessing the Causal Impact of Air Pollution on Childcare Outcomes in the U.S.
Working Paper (with Garth Heutel)
Links restricted CAPS administrative records from Georgia's Department of Early Care and Learning with satellite-derived pollution data. A one-unit increase in county-week average PM2.5 decreases childcare attendance by roughly two percentage points. Effects are larger for racial minorities and children with working parents.
The Economic Burdens of TRAP Restrictions: Effects on Housing and Childcare Costs
Working Paper
Uses staggered difference-in-differences over 2010–2019 to estimate whether targeted regulations on abortion providers impose broader financial costs on households beyond direct healthcare access.
