Mahta Ghafarianghadim

(headshot last updated: 2025)

I am a Ph.D. Candidate in Economics at the Andrew Young School of Policy Studies at Georgia State University, where I study how environmental exposures and policy changes affect health outcomes and economic wellbeing. My advisor is Professor Garth Heutel.

I use instrumental variables and difference-in-differences designs to identify these effects credibly, focusing on the populations who bear the largest costs of pollution and regulatory change.

In my job market paper, I estimate the causal effect of fine particulate matter (PM2.5) on cardiovascular mortality in the U.S. using thermal inversions as an instrument, finding that a one-unit increase in PM2.5 raises cardiovascular mortality by 3.8 percent, with the largest effects among Black, elderly, and low-income populations. I also study the health consequences of smoking, the effects of air pollution on early childhood childcare attendance, and the economic burdens of abortion access restrictions.

I have also served as instructor of record at Georgia State University, teaching Principles of Microeconomics and Environmental Economics and Policy, with strong student evaluations (see my Teaching page for details).
